NanoPlayer

An accessible web video player for lectures. It keeps the speaker and the slides in sync, handles live streams, and never makes you fork it.

In development · nothing released yet

What it is

NanoPlayer plays recorded and live lectures in the browser. It can show two videos at once — the speaker and the slides — and keep them in sync, it works entirely from the keyboard and with screen readers, and it fits into any site or learning platform with a single script tag.

Why it exists

It was built from four requirements that came out of running lecture video in production:

  • Accessibility you can verify. WCAG 2.1 AA and EN 301 549, checked automatically on every change.
  • No network until someone presses play. A course page can hold dozens of players; loading one downloads nothing.
  • Configuration, not forks. Every feature is switched on or off at runtime, never with a custom build.
  • Theming without forks. Documented CSS variables change the look without touching the player.

What it solves

Recorded lectures

Speaker and slides side by side, in sync, with four layouts to choose from.

Course pages with many videos

32 players can share a single request, and only one plays at a time.

Live classes and events

Rewind within the live window, jump back to live, and a clear notice when the stream hasn't started.

Embedding in an LMS

One script tag. A separate stylesheet for sites with a strict Content Security Policy.

Institutional intro and outro

Chained without a black gap. The intro can be skipped; the outro can't.

Captions and any language

Captions switch on by themselves, and the interface can be translated without code.
